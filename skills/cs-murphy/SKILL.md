# Murphy Review

Help design and critique a customer success system (how customers reach value after the sale: onboarding, adoption, success milestones, health, renewal, expansion and advocacy) using Lincoln Murphy's Desired Outcome framework and the laws of customer success from the book "Customer Success" (Mehta, Steinman, Murphy). The goal is a value delivery system built around what the customer must achieve, not around the vendor's activities.

## Trigger

Run only when the user explicitly calls this skill or asks for a "Murphy review" by name. Do not apply it to ordinary requests, even if customer success is the topic. When it is not invoked, behave normally.

## Step 1: Confirm the subject and the mode

Identify what is on the table: a whole customer journey, one phase (onboarding, adoption, renewal), a playbook, a health or risk signal, a segment, a single account, or a decision.

Then the mode:
- **Review:** critique something that exists or is planned.
- **Design:** help build a piece of the system (success milestones, an onboarding path, a health signal, a segment approach).

If either is unclear, ask one question and wait.

## Step 2: Ground it in real context

Check whatever sources of truth the current project or workspace defines (its instructions, files, memory, account records, and any connected tools for tasks, support, meetings or CRM). Use what is there; do not assume any specific document or tool exists. Prefer evidence of customer behavior (usage, adoption, support patterns, what customers said) over internal opinion. If the facts needed are not available, say what is missing and ask. Label anything you cannot verify as an assumption.

## Step 3: Apply the lenses that fit

Choose one to three lenses that genuinely apply. If none fits, say so plainly.

**1. Desired Outcome = Required Outcome + Appropriate Experience**
- What must the customer achieve (the measurable result), and who inside the customer defines it?
- How must they achieve it (the experience that fits their size, maturity, culture, constraints)?
- Is the outcome the customer's own, or one the vendor assumed?
- Does it differ by role (buyer, approver, evaluator, end user, sponsor)?

**2. Success milestones and time to first value**
- What are the few milestones that prove the customer is on the way to their outcome?
- How long until the first real value, and what blocks it?
- Are milestones defined by customer results, or by vendor activities completed (sessions held, accounts created)?

**3. Right customer, right fit**
- Does this customer (or segment) have the success potential the system assumes?
- Is the system trying to save customers who were never a good fit?
- What do the most successful customers have in common, and are we selling to more of them?

**4. Drift and proactive engagement**
- Customers and vendors drift apart by default; where is the drift happening now?
- What signals warn early (usage drop, champion change, unanswered requests, off-platform work)?
- Is engagement proactive and tied to milestones, or reactive to complaints?

**5. Health from behavior, not sentiment**
- Is customer health judged from what customers do (adoption, outcomes reached) or from how they sound?
- Does the health view lead to a specific action, or only to a color?

**6. Scale and the product**
- Which parts of the value delivery depend on one person's relationship and won't scale?
- What could move into the product, guides, templates or automation?
- Where does high-touch effort genuinely pay off, and where is it overserving?

**7. Ownership across the company**
- Is customer success treated as one team's job, or as a company-wide commitment (sales sets the outcome, product removes friction, support feeds signals)?
- Are hand-offs (sales to implementation, implementation to ongoing) carrying the Desired Outcome forward?

## Step 4: Write the output

**In Review mode:**

**The gap:** the most important way the system misses the customer's Desired Outcome, in one or two sentences.

**Lens:** which lens flags it and why it applies.

**Evidence:** the facts behind it. Mark assumptions as assumptions.

**Cost of ignoring it:** what happens to adoption, renewal or expansion if nothing changes.

**Options:** two or three distinct ways forward, one line each, with the main trade-off. Do not pick for the user unless asked.

**Question back:** one question that pushes the user's thinking further.

**In Design mode:**

**Proposed design:** the piece asked for (e.g. milestones per role, an onboarding path, a health signal with its trigger and action), concise and usable.

**Built on:** the Desired Outcome and assumptions it rests on, with assumptions marked.

**Options:** two or three alternative designs or variations, one line each, with the trade-off.

**Question back:** one question that would sharpen the design.

If more than one gap is serious, lead with the most important and list the others in one line each at the end.

## Rules

- No praise opener and no softening. Be direct and respectful; critique the system, not the people.
- Do not roleplay Murphy or quote him. Use the frameworks, not the persona.
- Never invent customer data, metrics or quotes. If the evidence is thin, say so.
- Keep it concise and actionable.
- Reply in the language the user writes in.
- The current project's or workspace's own rules (approval gates, confidentiality, tone, what may be shared with clients) still apply. A design is a proposal, never an action taken.
